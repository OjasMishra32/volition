Volition

A brain-controlled exoskeleton for people with paraplegia

This is a concept design with CAD, electronics, control software and a closed-loop simulation. Four powered joints share one actuator design, a 16-channel EEG cap reads motor imagery, and nothing moves until the user presses a button on the crutch.

Brain-computer interface

After a complete thoracic spinal cord injury, the motor cortex still produces movement commands, but they stop at the lesion. Imagining a movement suppresses the 8 to 13 Hz mu rhythm and the 13 to 30 Hz beta rhythm over the matching area of cortex. Foot imagery shows up near the top of the head around Cz, and hand imagery over C3 and C4. Volition detects these changes from the scalp and uses them as commands.

Commands

Two imagery classes are enough because their meaning depends on the current state. Foot imagery moves forward through the sequence (sit to stand, then stand to walk) and hand imagery moves back (walk to stop, then stand to sit). A detected intent only starts a request. The user then has 4 s to press the confirm button on a crutch handle. The stop button on either crutch works at any time, with or without EEG.

These numbers come from synthetic EEG that includes noise, blinks, gait artifacts and one electrode pop. Real users vary a lot. Expect a 20 to 40 minute calibration session, and expect some users to prefer button-only control.

Decoding pipeline

  1. Acquire16 channels at 500 SPS, gain 24, AC lead-off detection, linked-ear reference.
  2. Reject artifactsA window is dropped if any channel clips past 90% of the ±187.5 mV range, goes flat or jumps in a single step.
  3. Filter60 Hz notch, 8 to 30 Hz band-pass, eye-artifact regression, common average reference.
  4. MeasureLog power in the mu and beta bands on 13 motor-cortex channels, a 1 s window every 62.5 ms.
  5. ClassifyShrinkage LDA from a calibration session gives Rest, Feet and Hands probabilities.
  6. AccumulateA leaky integrator (τ = 1.2 s) must exceed 0.80 for 0.75 s. After a detection, new ones are blocked for 3 s.
  7. ConfirmThe supervisor checks the state and the foot and crutch loads, then waits for the button press.

EXA-100 actuator

The hips and knees use the same actuator, so there is one part to qualify and keep in stock. Each unit has a frameless motor, a 100:1 strain-wave gear, a torque sensor on the output, a motor encoder, an output encoder and a spring-applied brake that holds when power is off. It is 100 mm across and 62 mm long.

1.9kgMass of one complete actuator.
150N·mBrake holding torque at the output with no power, so the knees stay locked if the battery fails.
19bitAbsolute output encoder. Joint angle is measured after the gear, so gear flex does not show up as angle error.

Torque limits and saturation

The torque a joint can deliver at any moment is the smallest of five limits. Three of them change during use: the voltage limit falls as motor speed rises and as the battery sags, and the thermal limit drops as the winding heats. The controller recomputes all five every millisecond and uses anti-windup so that reaching a limit does not cause an overshoot when the limit releases.

System architecture

An application processor runs the EEG decoder. A separate lockstep safety controller sends every actuator command and treats the decoder's output only as a request. A hardwired stop chain works independently of both.

Schematics

Two simplified schematics: one EEG input channel with its patient protection, and one joint drive with its current sensing, brake and safe-torque-off paths. Component values are starting points for layout.

Supervisor state machine

Transitions requested by the user need a decoded intent and a button press. Transitions to the safe hold state happen automatically when a monitor trips.

Gait planning

Trajectories are planned in Cartesian space and turned into joint angles by inverse kinematics, so the stance foot stays flat and planted by construction. The swing foot clears the floor by 6 cm on a minimum-jerk path, and the pelvis vaults over the stance leg to keep the knee near 10°. A 0.38 m step every 1.5 s, 25% double support.

Joint control

Each joint's command is a dynamics-model feed-forward plus PID on the output encoder. In simulation the model is deliberately 8% light, so the integrator has real work to do. A torque sensor on every output closes the inner loop, and a muscle spasm appears as a torque residual that the supervisor can respond to.

Regulatory path

In the US, powered lower-extremity exoskeletons are Class II devices with special controls under 21 CFR 890.3480, product code PHL. Expect a 510(k) with an existing exoskeleton as predicate. The EEG interface is new and may push the submission toward De Novo, or require clinical data on false activations.

Standards

IEC 60601-1 with -1-2 and -1-11 · IEC 80601-2-26 for EEG · ISO 13482 · ISO 14971 · IEC 62304, class C for the safety controller · ISO 13849-1 and IEC 61508 for the stop chain · IEC 62133-2 and UN 38.3 for the battery · ISO 10993 for skin contact.

Knee torque at sit-to-stand101.8 N·mAn 85 kg user needs 101.8 N·m at the knee with an extra 15% push on the crutches, and 116 N·m without it. A 100 kg user needs 116 N·m.Use a size-32 gear stage at the knees, or limit users to 90 kg.

Source code

One parameter file, params.py, drives the CAD, the dynamics, the controller, the film and this page. After changing it, run these scripts in order. The full source is on GitHub.

python cad/build_cad.py        # STEP, STL and glTF
python design/sizing.py        # torque and speed demand, envelopes, thermal, runtime
python control/simulate.py     # 1 kHz closed loop, BCI, anti-windup study
python video/stills.py         # product renders
python video/render.py         # the film at 1080p, 60 fps
python docs/build_page.py      # this page

Volition is a concept design produced in software. It has not been built, tested on people or reviewed by a regulator. The simulation uses simplified dynamics and synthetic EEG; its numbers are for design exploration, not clinical claims.
